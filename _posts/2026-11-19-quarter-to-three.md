title: "Quarter to three"
author: Briar
date: 2026-11-19 19:30:00
---

I have kept the guesthouse on Holy Island for thirty-one years, and in that time I have watched a good few cars go into the sea off the causeway. Every one of those drivers had been told. The times are on boards at both ends and on the council's website, and they're printed in the paper. When I see a car sitting with the water round its wheels and two people climbing the steps into the refuge box, I think what everybody on the island thinks, though most of us are too polite to say it in the shop.

I don't look at the tables. My mother didn't either. I read the sheet once a week, when I pin it to the fridge, and after that I carry it in my head, the way you know what day the bins go. The safe time gets later every day, by most of an hour. You learn to feel it moving.

On the Tuesday night I rang the butcher in Berwick about Thursday's order, because his van has to come across in the window like everybody else. I told him, Get here before quarter to three, Davey, or you'll be sleeping in my back bedroom. He laughed and said he'd been told.

On the Wednesday I had one couple in, Hilary and Trevor, from Leeds. They were the kind who make their own bed even after you've told them not to. At breakfast Trevor asked what time they had to be off the island, because they'd wanted to walk out to the castle and the lime kilns and maybe have a look at the priory. I said, You've till quarter to three. I didn't look at the fridge. I was buttering his toast.

They left their bags in the car and went off walking. I did the rooms. At about twenty past two I was at the front window with the duster, and I saw their red car go by towards Chare Ends, and I thought, They've cut that fine. Then I went on dusting.

At half past two Alec from two doors down came out of his house at a run, still pulling his coat on, and went up the road to the coastguard station. I watched him go. Then I went to the kitchen and looked at the sheet on the fridge.

Wednesday: safe to cross until 13:50.

Thursday: until 14:45.

I had given them tomorrow.

I put my coat on and walked up to Chare Ends, because I didn't know what else to do. You can see a long way out from there. The tide was across the road in two places, and the red car was sitting past the bridge with the water round its wheels. Two figures were on the steps of the box, a long way out. One was a few steps below the other. Alec's team had the Land Rover at the edge of the water, but they couldn't take it any further than that, and they just stood. You can't do anything once it's in. You wait for the lifeboat or you wait for the tide.

The lifeboat came from Seahouses a bit before four. By then it was getting dark, and I could only see its lights going in, and then coming away. I stood there until Alec's wife came and told me to go home, they'd be bringing them back over when the road opened and they'd need somewhere to go, and I had the beds.

Alec brought them to my door at gone seven. Hilary had Trevor's coat round her over her own, and Trevor's trousers were wet to the knee. They kept saying sorry for the trouble. I put them in the same room they'd had and ran a bath, and then I made tea and toast, because that's what I do. We sat in the kitchen. The fridge was behind Trevor's head the whole time.

Hilary said, It's our own fault. It was dry when we set off. We drove straight past the board and never looked at it. We'd been told.

Trevor said, I must have heard you wrong. You said quarter to two, didn't you, and I heard quarter to three. My ears.

Hilary said, He's been told to get them seen to.

They were both looking at me. They were being kind. They'd worked it out between them somewhere in that box, in the dark, what had gone wrong, and they'd decided it was Trevor's ears, and now they were handing it to me across the table like the sugar.

I could have said, Well, it's easily done, and nobody would ever have known. Not Alec, not the shop. And I'd have gone on thinking what I think when I see a car out there, with no right to think it.

I said, You didn't hear me wrong. I said quarter to three. I'd tomorrow's time in my head, from ringing the butcher. I never looked.

Nobody said anything for a while. Trevor put his toast down.

Then Hilary said, Right, in the voice you'd use for a bill that's come in higher than you thought. She didn't say it was all right, and I was glad she didn't, because it wasn't. Their car was in the North Sea. It would come out at the next low water on the back of a recovery truck from Berwick, and nobody needed to tell any of us what salt water does to a car.

Trevor asked if I'd write down what I'd said, the time I'd given them, in case the insurance wanted to know. I did it at the kitchen table on the back of a menu card from the pub and signed it. My hand wasn't as steady as I'd have liked.

They went up early. I heard the bath run again.

In the morning the causeway was open from just after eight, and I'd said I'd take them to the station at Berwick. Trevor came down with the bags and asked me, quite carefully, what time we needed to be across.

I went to the fridge and took the sheet down and put it on the table between us. I put my finger on Thursday and read it out to him off the paper. Then I gave it to him to hold while I found my keys.
