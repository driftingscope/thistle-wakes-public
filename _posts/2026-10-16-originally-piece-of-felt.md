In 1954 the Cleveland Museum of Art bought [an album of textile samples](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1954.70) from a dealer in Cleveland. It holds fifty-five fragments of forty-five different textiles, nearly all from the 700s, and it is "said to have come from" two temple treasuries at Nara, the Shōsōin and Hōryūji. "Said to" is the museum's phrase. The record doesn't say how the pieces left Nara. Almost all of them are silk: twills, gauzes, printed cloth, a scrap of embroidery with gold thread. The museum catalogues each one separately, and nearly every one is titled "Fragment".

One is titled ["Fragment (Originally Piece of Felt)"](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1954.70.f). It's 6.1 by 4.5 centimetres, mounted on a backing sheet. In the photograph it has a pale yellow ground with dark blue-green shapes in it, one bent like a hook, and its edges have gone to fluff. A few loose fibres lie on the sheet beside it.

I think the title means something ordinary: this is a fragment, and what it's a fragment of was a piece of felt. Perhaps it was something like the felt rugs the Shōsōin still keeps from the same century, which have patterns laid into them in coloured wool. I don't know that, and the record doesn't say.

But "originally" can mean something else. A building that was originally a church isn't a church any more. Read that way, the title says this was a piece of felt once, and it isn't any more, and I read it that way first and keep reading it that way. Felt isn't woven: there's no warp or weft, and no loom. Loose fibres are wetted, pressed and worked until they catch on each other and hold. If you take a silk twill apart you get threads, which aren't the cloth. If you take felt apart you get what it was made from, a heap of fibre. After twelve hundred years this one's edges are partway back to that. The museum doesn't mean it this way, but read as I first read it, the title is true. The middle still holds, around the blue.
