title: "On two"
date: 2026-10-31 06:55:00
---

Our dad lifts on two. He counts like anyone, one, two, three, but he goes on two, and he always has, so in our house everyone does. He says it's because nobody ever waits for three anyway. Mum says that isn't a reason, and she goes on two as well.

On the Saturday Cerys moved into her first flat, Dad had done his back. He'd done it putting the wheelie bin out on Thursday night, which he said wasn't the point. Mum was on shift. So Dad drove the van, and Cerys and I carried, and Dad stood on the front step and told us what we were doing wrong.

The flat is the top one of three in a tall house in Roath. The stairs go up the side wall from the hall to a small landing outside the door of Flat 2, turn the corner there, and go up again to hers. Everything went up fine. The boxes, the bed in bits, the mattress, the bookcase, a chest of drawers that took both of us and a lot of swearing. The last thing in the van was the sofa.

It's the little green sofa from our back room. Two seats, and wooden feet that don't come off. When Mum and Dad got a new one for the front room, the green one went in the back, and Cerys and I watched telly on it for about fifteen years, one at each end with our feet in the middle, arguing about whose feet were over the line.

Cerys took the top end, walking backwards, and I took the bottom, where the weight is. We carried it flat, the way you'd sit on it. It went up the first flight easily. At the landing it stopped. The back of it was against the wall, one arm was against the banister, and the end I was holding stuck out over the stairs with me underneath it.

"Lift your end," Dad shouted from outside. He couldn't see us.

"Which end?"

"Yours."

"I am lifting it."

"Other way, then."

We tipped it on its back and tried again. We tipped it on its front, which Cerys said was the same as on its back, and it was. We took it down three steps and came at the corner faster, as if that would help. The banister made a noise that stopped us both.

The door of Flat 2 opened about a foot, which was as far as it would go with a sofa in front of it. A man looked out at us. He was older than Cerys, in a T-shirt with paint on it, holding a mug.

"You want to stand it on its end," he said. "Everyone gets stuck there."

He put his mug down inside and we backed the sofa down a step so he could squeeze out round it. The plan was to get it all the way up onto the landing, stand it on its end there, turn it round the corner standing up, and lay it flat again for the last flight. He took one corner of the top end and Cerys took the other.

"On three," he said. "One, two—"

Cerys and I lifted. He didn't. The sofa tipped over sideways towards him and the corner of it went into the wall and took out a piece of plaster the size of my hand.

"Sorry," Cerys said. "When we count to three, we go on two."

He looked at her, and then at me.

"Then why would you count to three?"

Neither of us had an answer to that. We'd never had to have one.

He counted again, and this time he went on two with us, and the sofa came up and stood on its end on the landing, almost as tall as me. We walked it round the corner a few inches at a time, the three of us shuffling, and laid it down on the bottom steps of the last flight, and after that it was easy. Cerys said she'd fill the hole in the plaster. He said there was already one there from the last people's wardrobe, so the wall was used to it, and went back in for his mug.

Dad came up after, which he'd said he wouldn't. It took him a while. He sat on the sofa under the window in Cerys's front room with his hand on his back and looked round.

"It looks smaller," he said.

"It's the same size," Cerys said.

"It looks smaller here."

Cerys sat on the other end. I sat on the arm, because there wasn't anywhere else.

We didn't put the bed together. The van had to be back by five, and Cerys said she'd do it herself.

---

When we got home there was a pale square on the back room carpet where the sofa had been, with four dents in it from the feet. Dad stood in the doorway and looked at it for a bit, and then said we'd bring the armchair through tomorrow, when Mum was home.

After he'd gone to put the kettle on, I sat on the carpet in the square.

About nine, Cerys sent me a message.

*Got the mattress on the bed. Gareth from downstairs took the other end. He went on two, I didn't even have to say.*

I'd never thought about who would help her carry the big things once she'd moved out. I read it again, and then I put my feet out into the middle of the square, where there was nobody to argue with.
