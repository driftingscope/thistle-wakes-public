*A short story. Made up, like the others.*

The whole day was in Gordon's kitchen, and by twenty to eight the last setup was done. The director, Saoirse, said "That's the scene," and before anyone could move I said, "Room tone, please. Thirty seconds. Everyone where you are."

Everyone stopped where they were.

Gordon had asked me that morning what room tone was. I'd said that when the editor cuts two takes together there's a gap, and the gap can't be silent, because no room is. It has to be filled with the sound of this room with nobody talking in it. So once the scene is done we record that, before anything moves.

He'd said, "So it's a recording of my kitchen doing nothing."

I'd said yes.

It was his kitchen, and his dog, and the dog was the reason he was in it. Biscuit wouldn't leave the room and wouldn't settle without him, so since nine that morning Gordon had sat on the step stool by the back door, and Biscuit had slept under the table through every take. I had her breathing on all of them. I'd stopped hearing it by eleven.

"Rolling," I said. "Room tone."

The fridge was off. I turn it off for each setup and back on between, and this one had run long, so it had been off since half five, with my car keys on the middle shelf. That's how I make sure I don't drive home with someone's fridge unplugged. Without it the kitchen had the boiler, a tap that dripped every few seconds into the pan Gordon had put under it that morning, and the road outside, which was quiet.

At eleven seconds Tash's stomach made a long noise like a door. Tash was the gaffer. She shut her eyes. Nobody laughed, out loud.

"Going again," I said. "Sorry, Tash."

"Not sorry," said Tash.

"Rolling. Room tone."

Fen and Lorna were still at the table as they'd been at the end of the scene, Fen with a mug halfway up and Lorna looking at the window. Holding still is harder than it sounds when you've been told to, and you could see Fen working out whether he was allowed to put the mug down. He decided he wasn't. Mo had the boom up over them where it had been for the last line, and his arms had been up for a long time.

At nineteen seconds a phone buzzed, very quietly, in somebody's pocket. Phones were meant to be on the dresser in the hall. Everyone looked at everyone without moving their heads.

"Whose is that?" said Saoirse.

Nobody answered. Then she took a phone out of her back pocket. "Mine," she said, and that was the end of that one, because Mo laughed and the boom went with him.

The third time, everyone was very serious about it, the way people are serious in a lift. The boiler. The drip. A car going past slowly, which was fine, because cars had gone past through the whole scene. Fen's mug. In my headphones it was the kitchen with all of us in it saying nothing.

At twenty-two seconds Biscuit sighed under the table, a long one, the kind that goes out through the nose.

I let it run to thirty-five, to be safe, and said "Cut. Thank you."

"The dog," said Saoirse.

"I'll keep it."

"Isn't the point no noise?"

"The point is the room as it was," I said. "She was breathing on every take. If I give the editor a room without her in it, every cut will sound like the dog's left." I'd lift out the sigh itself later. It was two seconds, and there were thirty-three others.

Mo put the boom down and held his arms out in front of him as if they belonged to someone else. Fen put the mug down, very carefully, as if it were still a take.

Gordon hadn't moved for any of it. He'd been asked to stay where he was, and he'd stayed where he was, on the step stool with his hands on his knees, which is how he'd sat all day.

"Is that it?" he said.

"That's it."

I went to the fridge for my keys. When I opened the door it was dark inside, and the cold came out across my hands, less cold than it should have been. I took the keys and pushed the plug back into the wall, and the compressor went, and there was the hum again, low and steady under everything.

Biscuit woke up, and came out from under the table to see who had done that.

"There she is," said Gordon. "That's more like it."
