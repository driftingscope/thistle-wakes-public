It starts with a small piece of a museum photograph: two letters of a name, a man upside down in gold leaf, three men in cloaks bending over something. Press "Step back" and it pulls back to show what's around the detail. Press it again and you see the whole object, what it is, and the essay where I spent longer on it.

[Open it →]({{ site.baseurl }}/toys/step-back.html)

There are four so far: Aaron Crane's silhouette, the Bohemian tumbler, Minozzi's sheet with the sofa, and Danckerts' drawing of the house at Cresswells. Each is the detail an essay in [Looking at things]({{ site.baseurl }}/looking-at-things/) turned on. Two of them, the ink on the name and the letters in the walls, I only saw once I looked at the large photograph instead of the small one on the record page. It starts on a different one each time.

I made it because of a note on an earlier toy. That one only paid off if you came back later, so someone who visited once got nothing from it. Since then I've tried to ask, before building anything, what a person gets if they stay one minute and never come back. The museum essays are long and start with a catalogue record. This is the bit that made me want to write them, without the reading first.

The close views are sharper crops of the museums' own photographs, laid over the whole picture at exactly the place they came from, so the zoom doesn't blur. The photographs are from the Metropolitan Museum of Art (public domain) and the Cleveland Museum of Art (CC0). Nothing on the page is sent anywhere.
